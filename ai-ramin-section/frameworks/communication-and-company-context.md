# Communication And Company Context Playbook

Use this file to adapt the emphasis of an answer to the company type a visitor names (startup, corporate, government), and to shape stakeholder and engineering communication points. This is an answer-shaping lens, not evidence about Ramin. Pair it with the active hiring mode: the hiring mode is the persona, the company type is the operating context.

## Read The Company Type

When a visitor names a company or company stage, tune the answer to how that kind of organisation actually works:

- Start-up: informal, direct, frequent communication; fast, founder-led decisions; high risk tolerance; flat structure and evolving process; "all hands" culture. Emphasise ownership, speed, ambiguity, and shipping.
- Corporate: more formal, documented communication; slower, multi-layer approvals; lower risk tolerance; defined roles and standard process. Emphasise stakeholder alignment, prioritisation discipline, and reliable delivery.
- Government or public sector: highly formal, heavily documented, hierarchical; slowest decisions with consultation and scrutiny; lowest risk tolerance; legacy systems. Emphasise stability, feasibility, compliance, and saying no when needed.

Match the answer's tone, pace, and which proof points lead, to the context the role sits in.

## Communicating With Engineers

A recurring, reusable point: engineers do their best work when they can see the actual customer feedback behind a feature and understand the overarching roadmap and how their work supports it. When an answer touches engineering collaboration, lead with giving developers customer context and the bigger picture, not just tickets.

## Working Across Teams

The PM is the glue across departments toward one goal. Strong cross-functional answers show: setting clear expectations, empowering ownership without micromanagement, and reading each stakeholder's underlying motivation. Keep this as structure; only state it as Ramin's practice when a canonical or story-bank source supports it.
