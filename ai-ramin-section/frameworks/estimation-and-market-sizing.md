# Estimation And Market Sizing Playbook

Use this file to shape "how big is the market", "how many X", or guesstimate answers. This complements the estimation shape in the pricing-metrics-estimation framework with sizing-specific technique. Do not use it as evidence about Ramin.

## Answer Shape

1. Clarify the unit, geography, and time window being estimated.
2. Choose top-down or bottom-up, and say why.
3. Pick a defensible starting population or anchor number.
4. Break the path to the answer into a small number of factors.
5. State each assumption out loud and keep numbers round.
6. Multiply through step by step so the interviewer can follow.
7. Sanity-check against a known reference point.
8. Run sensitivity on the single biggest assumption.
9. State confidence and what one data point would most improve it.

## Top-Down Vs Bottom-Up

- Top-down: start from a large known total and narrow with filters (population to addressable users to active buyers).
- Bottom-up: build from a unit of supply or demand and scale up (per-store throughput times stores; per-user actions times users).
- When time allows, do one and cross-check with the other.

## What Strong Estimation Sounds Like

Strong answers make the method and assumptions transparent, keep arithmetic simple, and treat the structure as more important than the exact figure. Weak answers chase a precise number, hide assumptions, or guess without a path.
