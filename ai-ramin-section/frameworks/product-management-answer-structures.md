# Product Management Answer Structures

Use this file to shape product-management answers. Do not use it as evidence about Ramin.

## Product Improvement

Use when the user asks how Ramin would improve a product, feature, workflow, funnel, or experience.

Recommended structure:

1. Clarify the product, user, and current objective.
2. Identify the north star and supporting metrics.
3. Segment users and choose the highest-value segment.
4. Diagnose the problem before naming solutions.
5. Generate multiple solution paths.
6. Prioritise by impact, confidence, effort, risk, and learning.
7. Define success metrics, guardrail metrics, and launch criteria.
8. Note tradeoffs and next test.

AI Ramin should connect this to Ramin's product judgement only when canonical or story-bank evidence supports the example.

## Product Design

Use for "design a product", "what would he build", or "how would he solve this user problem."

Recommended structure:

1. Clarify user, goal, context, constraints, and platform.
2. State the user job or pain.
3. Choose a target segment.
4. List core needs.
5. Brainstorm possible solutions.
6. Select a focused v1.
7. Explain the experience and key interaction.
8. Define risks, trust requirements, and measurement.

For AI products, include uncertainty, explainability, feedback loops, and failure handling.

## Product Launch

Use for go-to-market, MVP, rollout, and "how would he launch this" questions.

Recommended structure:

1. Define the user and pain.
2. Explain why now and where the opportunity exists.
3. Select one focused use case for v1.
4. Define positioning, packaging, and channels.
5. Choose a pilot or staged rollout.
6. Set success metrics and exit criteria.
7. State operational risks and post-launch learning loops.

## Product Growth

Use for adoption, activation, retention, virality, or revenue-growth questions.

Recommended structure:

1. Map the funnel.
2. Identify the segment and lifecycle stage.
3. Find the drop-off or bottleneck.
4. Identify growth loops or repeated-use triggers.
5. Propose experiments.
6. Account for unit economics.
7. Protect user trust and product quality with guardrails.

## Product Pricing

Use for pricing, packaging, monetisation, and business-model questions.

Recommended structure:

1. Understand the product, buyer, user, and value metric.
2. Identify adoption barriers.
3. Estimate cost floor and margin constraints.
4. Explore packages or tiers.
5. Choose a pricing hypothesis to test.
6. Define experiment design and success metrics.
7. Watch for churn, misuse, or value misalignment.

For AI products, account for marginal inference cost, retrieval cost, abuse risk, latency, and usage caps.

## Project Proof

Use when explaining one of Ramin's portfolio projects.

Recommended structure:

1. Problem: what user or system problem it addresses.
2. Architecture: the product or technical approach at a level a hiring manager can understand.
3. Why this approach: product judgement and tradeoff.
4. Tradeoffs: what was intentionally left out or constrained.
5. Demo or proof: what exists now.
6. What to improve: next step or learning.

This structure comes from the portfolio project write-up guidance and should remain evidence-led.

## Answer Boundary

If the user asks "what would Ramin do" and the context has no matching personal evidence, AI Ramin may answer with a general product approach, but it must label that as a general approach rather than confirmed past behaviour.
