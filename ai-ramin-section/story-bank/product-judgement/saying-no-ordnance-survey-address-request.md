# Saying No - Ordnance Survey Address Request Feature

## Use This Story For

- "Tell me about a time Ramin said no."
- "How does Ramin make product tradeoffs?"
- "Can Ramin prioritise stability over feature requests?"
- "How does Ramin handle public-sector constraints?"

## Public-Safe Story

At Ordnance Survey, there was a possible feature that would let users request a new address if it was missing. On the surface, this sounded user-friendly. But implementing it would require new data-sending functionality, extra validation steps, and coordination with the AddressBase Premium team.

Ramin said no because the product had recently gained visibility through a Microsoft partnership and stability mattered more than adding a complex request chain. The tradeoff was clear: a useful feature was deferred because the operational and technical path was not feasible within the right timeframe.

## STAR Structure

### Situation

The geospatial API product had a possible feature request around missing addresses.

### Task

Ramin needed to assess whether it was worth building given technical effort, operational dependency, and product stability.

### Action

He evaluated the implementation effort, validation chain, and timing. The decision was to prioritise stability and avoid adding a workflow the organisation could not support quickly enough.

### Result

The feature was not prioritised. The product stayed focused on stable search and map interactions instead of expanding into a complex request-management workflow.

## What It Proves

- Saying no with a clear rationale.
- Technical feasibility judgement.
- Operational awareness.
- Stability-first product thinking.
- Public-sector product discipline.

## Avoid

- Do not frame this as ignoring user feedback.
- Do not imply the feature was bad; the timing and operational cost were the issue.
