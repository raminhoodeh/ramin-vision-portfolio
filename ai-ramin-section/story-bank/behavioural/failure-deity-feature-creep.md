# Failure - Deity AI Feature Creep

## Use This Story For

- "What is Ramin's biggest failure?"
- "Tell me about a product lesson Ramin learned."
- "How does Ramin handle senior stakeholder pressure?"
- "How did Ramin learn to avoid feature creep?"

## Public-Safe Story

Early in his product career at Deity AI, Ramin was too willing to accept senior stakeholder feature requests. The product had two real differentiators: Bluetooth matching and a chatbot conversation mediator. But saying yes too often diluted focus and made the roadmap harder to connect back to the core value proposition.

The lesson was that politeness is not the same as product leadership. A PM has to protect focus, even when the request comes from someone senior. After that experience, Ramin became much more deliberate about testing every feature request against strategy, user value, and the product's core differentiator.

## SOAR Structure

### Situation

Deity AI was an early-stage social product with a chatbot-mediated conversation experience and Bluetooth matching as the main differentiators.

### Obstacle

Ramin was eager to prove himself and accepted too many senior stakeholder requests without challenging whether they served the product strategy.

### Action

The learning was to create a stronger prioritisation filter: every request needed to map to user value, strategic alignment, and the core differentiator.

### Result

The experience shaped his later PM approach: sharper roadmap discipline, more assertive stakeholder communication, and stronger focus on the "one thing" that makes a product matter.

## What It Proves

- Reflective product leadership.
- Ability to learn from failure.
- Roadmap discipline.
- Stakeholder pushback.
- Feature prioritisation under pressure.

## Avoid

- Do not blame individual stakeholders.
- Do not overstate causal certainty around fundraising or company outcome.
- Do not present this as a current weakness; frame it as an early-career lesson that changed his PM style.
