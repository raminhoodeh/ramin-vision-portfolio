# Conflict - Urgentem Pricing Model

## Use This Story For

- "Tell me about a conflict at work."
- "How does Ramin handle disagreement with sales?"
- "How does Ramin think about pricing?"
- "Can Ramin balance commercial and product considerations?"

## Public-Safe Story

At Urgentem, there was a disagreement about how to price the ESG platform. A usage-based model would connect revenue directly to portfolio analyses, but Ramin was concerned it could discourage clients from exploring the full product.

Ramin argued for a tiered model because it would let clients use more of the platform inside a clear package, which would create better usage data, reveal workflow patterns, and support long-term product improvement. He listened to the commercial argument, framed the product risk, and connected the pricing decision back to adoption and learning.

The team landed on a tiered model with a higher initial company limit, according to the interview draft.

## STAR Structure

### Situation

Urgentem needed to decide how to price its ESG platform.

### Task

Ramin needed to advocate for a model that supported product adoption and learning while respecting sales concerns about revenue.

### Action

He listened to the sales team's usage-based argument, explained the risk of discouraging product exploration, used competitor and product-learning logic, and reframed pricing as a driver of product insight as well as revenue.

### Result

The team aligned on a tiered pricing model with a higher initial company limit. The story demonstrates pricing judgement, cross-functional communication, and the ability to make product-learning arguments in commercial terms.

## What It Proves

- Stakeholder empathy.
- Pricing strategy.
- Long-term product thinking.
- Ability to speak the language of sales without surrendering product judgement.
- Data-driven product learning.

## Avoid

- Do not present the sales team as wrong.
- Do not state exact pricing tiers unless retrieved from the canonical Urgentem file and verified.
- Do not imply that pricing alone drove product success.
